# attest v0 design

Status: Draft for review. No code exists yet; this document fixes the domain
model, contracts, and boundaries before any is written.

## Purpose

Identity providers answer "who authenticated?" with a credential. An agent
operating in the physical world rarely has one. It has evidence: a face match,
a voiceprint, a gait signature, a re-identified track. That evidence is
probabilistic, perishable, and sometimes wrong.

attest turns that evidence into an authorization input. It fuses calibrated
signals into a **Belief** about who a perceived person is, classifies the Belief
into tenant-defined **assurance bands**, and answers one question for an agent:

> May this agent perform this action, on behalf of the person it believes it is
> serving, right now?

The answer composes with the authorization stack a tenant already runs: roles
from an identity provider, relationships from a fine-grained authorization
engine. attest adds the missing dimension, which is how sure anyone is about
the human in the loop.

## Laws

These are invariants. Every contract below is shaped by them.

1. **Identification, not verification.** Evidence is open-set 1:N. `unknown` is
   a successful, first-class result and the common case.
2. **A Belief is never a credential.** It is transient and supersedable. Nothing
   attest emits may outlive the evidence that justified it.
3. **Ambiguity resolves to unknown.** Contested or conflicting evidence yields
   no leading identity. attest never grants on a coin flip.
4. **Freshness is the session.** There is no login. Access holds while evidence
   is fresh and lapses on its own.
5. **attest holds no biometrics.** Templates, vectors, images, and waveforms stay
   in the modality that produced them. attest sees identity UUIDs and calibrated
   confidences only.
6. **Policy is downstream of perception.** Roles, grants, and bands never
   influence who attest believes is present.
7. **The floor does not depend on identification.** Tenants can mark capability
   as available to anyone, including unknown subjects. Safety-critical function
   must not fail because a camera was occluded.
8. **Every decision explains itself.** A decision carries the trail of each
   predicate checked.
9. **The identity UUID is the only durable join.** Names and aliases are
   presentation. No external system's label is ever a key.

## Domain model

Dependencies point inward: each context depends only on the language of those
below it.

```
  Decision      agent + subject + delegation + permission -> Decision
     |
  Assurance     Band: a named predicate over Belief
     |
  Perception    Evidence -> Subject association -> Fusion -> Belief
     |
  Identity      Identity {id}, external principal links, consent
```

### Identity

```
Identity
  id            UUID, immutable
  displayName   presentation only
  aliases[]     names with provenance
  links[]       ExternalPrincipal {provider, subject}
  consent       who enrolled this identity and on what basis
```

An Identity is a person attest may perceive. It is not necessarily a principal
in any identity provider: a site can know a visitor without the visitor holding
an account. A link to an external principal is optional and one-way, and it is
the route by which provider roles and engine relationships attach to a
perceived person.

### Perception

```
SubjectRef               a perception lifetime (one track, one utterance)
  key                    opaque, source-bound
  context                opaque tenant labels (site, zone)

Evidence                 submitted by a modality
  subject                SubjectRef
  modality               "face" | "voice" | "gait" | ...
  source                 producer incarnation
  observedAt, expiresAt
  state                  resolved | unknown | ambiguous | invalid
  candidates[]           {identityId, confidence}
  calibration            version of the producer's calibration artifact

Association              "these two SubjectRefs are the same someone"
  a, b                   SubjectRef
  confidence, observedAt, expiresAt

Belief                   fused posterior about one subject
  subject
  state                  resolved | unknown | ambiguous | conflicted
  candidates[]           {identityId, confidence, modalities[]}
  leading                identityId | null
  confidence
  modalities[]           distinct modalities supporting the leader
  freshestAt             newest evidence that supports the leader
```

Fusion rules, carried over from the reference implementation this design
descends from:

- Evidence combines only across subjects that an Association has joined. Two
  signals in the same room are not the same person until something measured
  says so. A band that requires two modalities cannot clear from unrelated
  subjects.
- One slot per (subject, modality, source). New evidence supersedes old in its
  slot, including `unknown` superseding a prior match.
- A modality contributes its single strongest source. Three cameras are one
  modality, not three corroborations.
- Distinct modalities combine by noisy-or. This assumes modality errors are
  independent, which is an approximation; see open questions.
- A runner-up within the ambiguity margin, or producers that disagree on the
  leader, yields `leading = null`.
- Expired or retired evidence disappears. A reused track or source inherits
  nothing.

**Calibration is the conformance requirement.** A raw cosine from one model and
a raw cosine from another are not the same unit. A modality must map its scores
to calibrated confidence against a measured genuine/impostor distribution and
declare the version of that mapping. Evidence with missing or stale calibration
is treated as unknown.

### Assurance

```
Band
  name
  minConfidence?       leading confidence
  maxAge?              age of freshestAt
  minModalities?       distinct agreeing modalities
  coPresence?[]        roles that must also be believed present in context
```

Band names and thresholds are tenant vocabulary. attest evaluates them and
never interprets them. An empty band holds for anyone.

`coPresence` covers supervised access: a scope that holds only while a second
role (a caregiver, an attending, a supervisor) is also believed present.

Band evaluation sits behind a `BandPolicy` interface. v0 ships static
thresholds. The same interface later admits bands that tighten or relax with
live signal conditions, with no change to callers.

### Decision

A decision has three parties, which is what distinguishes this from
conventional access control:

```
Agent        the workload asking. Strongly authenticated by the identity
             provider (client credentials, workload identity).
Subject      the perceived human. Held as a Belief, never authenticated.
Delegation   the standing consent that lets this agent act for this identity.

Delegation
  identityId
  agent             client id or agent class
  permissions[]     what the agent may do on this identity's behalf
  ceiling?          strongest band this delegation can ever satisfy
  constraints?      context labels where it applies
  grantedBy, grantedAt, expiresAt, revokedAt
```

A request is granted only if all of the following hold:

1. the agent is authenticated and permitted to ask for this permission;
2. the permission is a floor permission, **or**
3. the Belief has a leading identity, and
4. a live Delegation covers (identity, agent, permission), and
5. the authorization engine says the identity holds the permission on the
   resource, and
6. the band the permission requires holds on the Belief.

```
Decision
  granted
  identityId | null
  band
  reasons[]          one line per predicate checked
  validUntil         earliest instant any satisfied predicate lapses
```

`validUntil` is derived, not configured. It is the moment the freshness
predicate fails if no new evidence arrives. Callers that cache a decision or
mint a downstream token must not exceed it.

## Composing with identity providers and FGA

attest is not an identity provider and not an authorization engine. It sits in
front of both through two ports.

### PrincipalProvider

```
verifyAgent(token) -> AgentPrincipal
listPrincipals() -> ExternalPrincipal[]
effectiveRoles(principal) -> string[]
```

Adapters: Keycloak first, then Ory and Auth0. The interface is deliberately
narrow so a provider is an adapter and nothing above it changes. Provider
outage does not affect identification; decisions fall back to the last durably
mirrored role state or deny, per tenant policy.

### AuthorizationEngine

```
check(principal, permission, resource, context) -> allow | deny
```

Two kinds of implementation:

- **Built-in RBAC.** Roles map to permissions in tenant config. Zero external
  dependencies; the default for a first self-hosted install.
- **External engines.** OpenFGA, Ory Keto, SpiceDB, Auth0 FGA.

The band requirement is attached to the permission **in attest**, and attest
conjoins the band result with the engine's answer. This works with any engine,
including those with no notion of conditional relationships. Where an engine
does support request-time conditions, an adapter may additionally pass the
assurance context (`band`, `confidence`, `ageMs`, `modalities`) so the tenant
can express band requirements natively in their authorization model. That
pushdown is optional and per-adapter; engine capabilities need to be verified
when each adapter is built.

### Tokens and continuous evaluation

The agent-identity work emerging from identity providers is token-shaped:
on-behalf-of exchange, delegated scopes. attest's second law says a Belief is
not a credential. The two reconcile as follows:

- The primary interface is a per-action decision call against the live Belief.
- Where a downstream system needs a token, attest may issue an **assurance
  assertion**: short-lived, audience-bound, carrying band and modalities (in
  the spirit of OIDC `acr`/`amr`), with expiry no later than `validUntil`.
- When a Belief decays, is contested, or is superseded by a different identity,
  attest emits a revocation event so holders can drop what they cached.

Assertions and revocation events are post-v0. The decision API is designed so
they are additions, not changes.

## API surface (v0)

```
PUT    /v1/identities/{id}              registry CRUD
POST   /v1/identities/{id}/links        link an external principal
POST   /v1/delegations                  create / revoke / list delegations

POST   /v1/evidence                     a modality submits Evidence
POST   /v1/associations                 a modality submits an Association
POST   /v1/retirements                  a source, subject, or identity is retired
GET    /v1/subjects/{key}/belief        current Belief

POST   /v1/decisions                    {subject, permission, resource} -> Decision
                                        (agent comes from the bearer token)

GET    /v1/events                       audit and state-change stream
```

The contract is defined once (OpenAPI) and SDKs are generated from it. Modality
producers and agents authenticate separately and hold disjoint permissions: a
producer can submit evidence and cannot ask for decisions; an agent can ask for
decisions and cannot submit evidence.

## Privacy

- No biometric material enters attest (law 5).
- Evidence is held in memory for its lifetime and is not persisted by default.
- Audit records carry the decision, band, and reasons. Whether they carry the
  identity UUID is tenant-configurable; telemetry and logs never do.
- Deleting an identity retires its evidence, removes its delegations and role
  projections, and emits an event that each modality must acknowledge.
  Unacknowledged deletions stay visibly incomplete.

## Implementation

**Go for the server.** The service is an I/O-bound decision and control plane
whose value is in its integrations, and the ecosystem it integrates with is Go:
OpenFGA can be embedded or called natively, providers and engines ship
first-party Go clients, and workload identity and policy-expression libraries
are mature. It builds to one static binary, which is what a self-hosted install
wants. Rust would be the better choice if fusion had to run on constrained
edge devices or in WASM. Fusion is a small in-memory state machine, so it is
kept as a pure package with no I/O, which leaves a later port open without
paying for it now.

**TypeScript for the console** and for the generated TS SDK. A Python SDK is
generated for modality authors, who mostly live there.

**Storage.** Postgres for identities, links, delegations, tenant policy, role
mirrors, and audit. Live perception state (evidence, associations, beliefs) is
in-memory.

**Layout.**

```
cmd/attest/              the binary
internal/identity/       registry, links, consent
internal/perception/     evidence, association, fusion (pure)
internal/assurance/      bands, BandPolicy
internal/decision/       the three-party evaluator
internal/provider/       PrincipalProvider + adapters
internal/authz/          AuthorizationEngine + built-in RBAC + adapters
internal/store/          Postgres
api/                     OpenAPI contract
sdk/                     generated clients
console/                 admin UI
```

**Tenancy.** Every persisted row and every in-memory key carries a tenant id
from the first commit. The open-source build runs one tenant by default.

## Open source and hosted

| | Open source (self-hosted) | Hosted |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence ingest, fusion, bands, decisions | yes | yes |
| Built-in RBAC, provider and engine adapters | yes | yes |
| Single-tenant console | yes | yes |
| Multi-tenant control plane, org management, SSO | | yes |
| Audit retention, search, and analytics | basic stream | yes |
| Calibration drift monitoring | | yes |
| Dynamic bands | interface only | yes |
| Edge fusion nodes managed from a central control plane | | yes |

The hosted topology keeps fusion at the customer's edge and sends decisions and
audit upstream, so raw evidence does not have to leave the site.

## Non-goals

- Implementing biometric matchers. attest defines the evidence contract;
  modalities are separate projects.
- Being an identity provider or issuing login sessions.
- 1:1 verification of a presented claim. A device or client asserting "I am X"
  is not evidence.
- Merging identities by similarity. Only an operator assigns or merges.

## Milestones

- **M0. Core.** Contracts, identity registry, fusion, bands, built-in RBAC,
  decisions with reasons and `validUntil`. In-memory and Postgres stores.
- **M1. Agents.** Agent authentication, delegations, Keycloak adapter.
- **M2. FGA.** OpenFGA adapter with optional context pushdown; Ory adapter.
- **M3. Console.** Identities, delegations, band tuning, decision audit.
- **M4. Signals.** Assurance assertions, revocation events, dynamic bands.

## Open questions

1. **Bands as error rates.** A band stated as "false-accept rate at most 1 in
   10,000" is more defensible than "confidence at least 0.75". Calibration
   makes this possible per modality, but a fused error rate depends on the
   independence assumption in noisy-or. Should v0 bands be confidence
   thresholds with error-rate bands as a later `BandPolicy`, or should the
   fusion model be made rigorous first?
2. **Presentation attacks.** `minModalities` is a weak stand-in for liveness. Do
   modalities report a liveness or attack-detection signal as part of Evidence,
   and can a band require it?
3. **Where delegations live.** attest owns them in this draft. Some providers
   are building consent and delegation records for agents; mirroring those may
   be better than a second source of truth.
4. **Agent classes.** Is a delegation made to one agent instance, to a client
   id, or to a class of agents? This decides how much the agent's own identity
   model needs to carry.
5. **Evidence transport.** HTTP is enough for v0. High-rate producers (per-frame
   tracks) may need a streaming ingest.
